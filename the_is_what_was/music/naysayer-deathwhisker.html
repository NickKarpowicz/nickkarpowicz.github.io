Big Orange Crayon

musical things

The Naysayer
Deathwhisker
Carrot Top Records

The most clever thing about this album is how it actively tries not to grab your attention. Anna Padgett's singing voice is soft, steady, and even seemingly bored at times, and the instrumentation is equally laid back. It's there, but it blends in with its surroundings. As time goes on, though, things begin to stick out at you: the chorus of "Currency", the bizarre lyrics of "Anna Silver School" (which were written by elementary school children), and you begin remembering more and more of it, until you realize that you have the whole album memoriezed and it hasn't left your CD player in a month. The songs that once blended in with the background now seem sharp, distinct and full of such bizarre nuances that you wonder where you were when you listened to it the first time. Well, if you're me anyway.

Most of the songs consist of guitar, drumming and vocals, with the occaisional violin appearance, and are somewhat reminiscient of Tara Jane O'Niel's work (which makes sense considering that Tara plays guitar on some of the songs), and fits nicely with Cynthia Nelson's other work in Retsin. It's all simultaneously stark and raw, but pretty. The lyrics are very direct and honest, they're especially nice on "Woman on 11th Street" and "Sick", and they force you to look beyond the delivery into lines like "we walk in a heart shape/we talk in a twist/as sticky as duct tape/we're missing the gist" ("Woman on 11th Street").

So yeah, this album is as brilliant as it is understated and it's one of the best things I've put in my stereo this year.

Total score: 684 out of 684
