Ramble
Yeah, Whatever
Ultra Groove

Ramble is another one of those weird projects that it really one person going under the guise of a band (although there are lots of other people who play instruments on this one). But in this case, that one person (Adam Bailine), is the singer/songwriter/main instument player and the person running the label on which this is the debut release.

So that aside, the music is mostly that kind of alternarock powerpop that you hear on the radio a lot and see on MTV (you know, the kind with the videos where the band is playing in some apartment building or warehouse and the singer is singing to some woman who is out running around on the streets in a weird outfit and acting all symbolic). But in this case, the songs manage to rock in several different ways: in the mainstream alternaive rock way, the 70's rock way and the oldies way, which is actually pretty cool. Actually, my favorite song is not one of those: it's called "Selina" and has a sweeter, simpler, kinda 60's-ish feel to it, which is probably why I like it. But even for the other ones, this is better than the other music in that category, if not because it's indie, because it varies more, isn't as textbook and manages to be interesting despite the constraints of it's genre. And it has heart beyond what's in the music, too: the profits from the album go to a music education program, as did the profits from a benefit concert. So this is what the stuff on the radio would be like if it was a force for good instead of evil.

There are some things that trouble me that are in the press kit, though: the label is part of an advertising company (but one that was founded and run by the same Adam Bailine, who seems to be one of the busier people alive today), and the songs were extensively tested on focus groups. I mean, you release music because it's good, not because it tests well in a good demographic, right? Right?!? At least my favorite wasn't one of the test group favorites, that would be creepy. But I do give the label mad props for the fact that the press kit smells mysteriously like Froot Loops.
