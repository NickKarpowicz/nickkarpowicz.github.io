Fear of Commitment
Paper Dolls and Paper Plates
dren records

Hooray for melancholy but beautiful pop music. Not many other kinds of music can seem familiar without being played out or classic without being retro. Fear of Commitment pulls it off perfectly, making slow but catchy, female-fronted indie-pop along the lines of a mellow, folkier Marine Research. "Wrong Face" in particular is an especially elegant pop song with great harmonies and a wistful-but-catchy chorus that I'm happy to have jump into my head unexpectedly. The whole album is in that vein and if you like such things, you'll have nothing to complain about with this album: timeless songwriting along the lines of Belle and Sebastian (minus Stewart's voice and the awful parts) that sounds familiar without being stale (as exemplified by creative songs like "Michael") and an overall sad-but-not-pathetic feel make it one of those albums that will never end up on the shelf and dusty.
